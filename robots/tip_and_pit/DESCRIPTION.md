## 1. Overall description in one paragraph

Tip and Pit are two **tiny "rolling-pin" robots**: each is a **flat white rectangular box**, about **60 mm long, 32 mm deep and 22 mm tall**, with a **large white drum wheel at each end** (axle along the box's long axis). The wheels are taller than the box (about Ø 30 mm), so each robot can drive either way up. The body is built as a **sandwich of flat white plates** (a lid, two or three frame layers and a base), held by **four small screws in the lid corners**. The lid is decorated by hand with a **black marker**: one half is coloured solid black with a **jagged, saw-tooth (flame / shark-tooth) border** and the other half is white with the robot's name in black letters, written **T, I, P** or **P, I, T** in a loose scrawl. On the front of each body hangs a **thin wedge flap** reaching down to the floor: **clear plastic** on Tip, **aluminium** on Pit. The flap is fixed along the body's front edge with **clear tape**, acting as a hinge, so it drops to the floor whichever way up the robot lands.

### 2.1 Body (BD): lid (LD), frame layers (FR), bottom plate (BP)
- **Shape**: a rectangular box, **60 mm long (X) × 32 mm deep (Y) × 22 mm tall (Z)**, z ∈ [4, 26] **(guess for the ground clearance)**. The corners are square, with small radii (r ≈ 1 mm).
- **Layering** (AWS62, front faces): the front face shows **4–5 horizontal bands**: the **lid (about 3 mm)** on top, then **two or three white frame layers (about 5 mm each)** with visible seams, and a **bottom plate (about 3 mm)**. The edges of the layers are slightly misaligned (each layer's edge is offset by up to 0.5 mm), which is typical of **stacked laser-cut or printed plates**. The layers are white; the lid's top face is white plastic.
- **Lid LD**: a flat plate **60 × 32 × 3 mm**, with a **raised rim about 1 mm wide** round its edge (AWS62 shows a lighter border line round the black area, suggesting the decoration sits in a shallow recess or the lid has a chamfered edge).
- **Screws S-LD**: **4 small silver Phillips pan/countersunk screws**, head Ø ≈ **3–3.5 mm** (M2), one in each lid corner, about **4 mm** in from each edge. They pass through the lid and frame layers into the bottom plate (or into nuts), clamping the whole sandwich **(guess)**.

### 2.3 Wheels (WH-L, WH-R) and hubs (HB)
- **Shape**: **thick drums, Ø ≈ 30 mm × 12 mm wide**, at each end of the body, axle along **X** through the body's centre at **y ≈ 0, z ≈ 15**.
- **Tyre**: **white**, **smooth**, slightly **matt and soft-looking** (white TPU print, or white foam/rubber). - **WG-T (Tip)**: a **clear, slightly frosted plastic sheet** (polycarbonate or PET, about **0.8–1 mm**), about **54 mm wide (X) × 30 mm long (Y)**, a little narrower than the body. Its rear edge is fixed to the body's **lower front edge** with **clear tape** (a shiny strip of tape about 10 mm wide along the joint). It runs down at about **22°** to the floor. Its front edge is straight; the corners are square with slight bends.

In that photo the flap stands **steeply up (about 60°)** because the robot is lying on its side or inverted in the box. ## 6. Mechanism
- **Drive**: 2WD **in line with the body's long axis**: the two wheels are at the ends, so each robot is a **two-wheel "rolling pin"** that drives in the ±Y direction and steers by differential speed. With no third contact point, the body would rock about the axle. The **wedge flap resting on the floor** is the third contact and stops the body rotating: when the motors drive, the reaction torque presses the flap down (or lifts it), so it is also a **skid**.
- **Weapon**: none; a pure wedge pusher. The cluster works as a pair.
- **Invertibility**: the wheels are taller than the body both ways and the flap is hinged, so when flipped the flap swings over to touch the floor on the new underside.

## 7. Assembly order (suggested rebuild)
1. Print/cut the **bottom plate**, **2–3 frame plates** (with motor, battery and electronics pockets) and the **lid**.
2. Lay the **two N20 motors** coaxially in the frame pockets, shafts out through the end walls.
3. Add the battery, the ESC/RX board and the switch in the remaining pockets; wire them.
4. Stack the plates and fix with **4 × M2 screws** through the corners.
5. Press the **drum wheels** onto the motor shafts.
6. Tape the **wedge flap** along the body's lower front edge.
7. Decorate the lid with the black marker (jagged half and letters).

## 14. Why the designer did it (per part; adversarial lens first)

- **Two small robots instead of one**: a cluster is hard to pin down. While one is under attack, the other can push the opponent. An opponent's weapon can only hit one at a time, and each robot is a small target.
- **Rolling-pin layout (wheels at the ends)**
  - Adversarial: the wheels are the biggest parts and form the robot's ends, so side hits land on soft tyres that absorb energy and roll the robot away rather than breaking the body. Being taller than the body, the wheels make the robot fully invertible: flipping it achieves nothing.
  - Getting under: the low body plus a floor-touching flap gets under opponents from the front.
  - Construction: two coaxial motors, the simplest drive possible.
- **Sandwich body with 4 corner screws**: plates are quick to make and replace; the stack clamps the motors without separate brackets; 4 screws give full access in seconds.
- **Hinged wedge flap (tape hinge)**
  - Getting under: a thin flexible sheet on the floor gets under opponents' edges. Hinged, it **always** lies on the floor, whichever way up the robot is, and it also follows bumps in the arena floor.
  - Adversarial: a spinner hitting the flap just folds it back; little energy goes into the body. Clear polycarbonate (Tip) is nearly unbreakable; aluminium (Pit) is stiffer and has a sharper edge.
  - It also acts as the third contact that stops the body spinning about the axle.
- **Big soft white drum wheels**: grip, shock absorption, invertibility. No guards are needed because the wheels are the outer shell.
- **Marker decoration**: quick, cheap identity; the two robots are visually paired (TIP / PIT, mirrored patterns), which helps the driver tell them apart in the arena.
