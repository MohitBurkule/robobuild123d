## 1. Overall description in one paragraph

For Box Sake is a **low, wide, four-wheel wedge** shaped like a flat box with a chamfered front. The body is about 76 mm wide between the wheel notches, about 84 mm long and 26 mm tall, with a **flat top** carrying **four domed screw heads** near its corners. The front is a **two-facet wedge**: an **upper steep face** dropping from the top's front edge to a crease about 10 mm above the floor, and a **lower shallow lip** that runs from the crease down to a sharp edge on the floor and **flares outward to the full width of the robot** (about 100 mm), covering the front wheels. Four **black rubber wheels**, wide and squat (about Ø 26 × 18 mm), sit in **rectangular notches** at the four corners of the box, their tops level with the top face. The whole robot is **wrapped in clear packing tape**, scratched all over; underneath, the translucent silver-grey print shows a dark **triangular rib lattice**. Two round **screw holes** sit in the upper front face, a **small white square** marks the top, and a **vertical seam** runs down the middle of the lower lip.

## 2. Part-by-part description

### 2.1 Main box shell (BX)
- **Shape**: a rectangular box, **76 mm wide (X, between the wheel notches) × 84 mm long (Y) × 26 mm tall (Z)** to the top face, with the front edge cut away by the two front facets (UF, LF). The vertical edges are sharp; the top edges have a small radius (about 2 mm).
- **Material**: **translucent silver-grey PLA/PETG**, printed hollow with a thin skin (about 1 mm) over a **triangular internal rib lattice RB**: dark purple/grey lines forming large triangles (side about 25–30 mm), visible through the skin on the top and the front faces.
- **Taping TA**: every visible face is covered in **clear packing tape** (layers overlapping), giving a glossy, heavily scratched surface; the tape edges show as faint straight lines (e.g. the horizontal line across the lower lip at about z = 12). The tape protects the print from scraping and makes the surface slippery.

### 2.2 Top face (TP)
- **Shape**: flat, **76 × 64 mm** visible (the front 20 mm is the upper front facet). Its front edge, at y ≈ +22, is a straight line across the width.
- **Screws SC-1…4**: **four domed (button-head) screws**, head Ø about **6 mm** (M3 button heads or large rivets), silver, near the corners: front pair at about (±30, +16), rear pair at about (±28, −36) (in the photo: front pair at (465, 250) and (1555, 250), rear pair at (515, 125) and (1500, 125)). They fix the top to the box walls or to internal posts **(guess)**.
- **Tag SQ**: a **small white square**, about **4 × 4 mm**, sitting on the top just behind the front edge at x ≈ −12 (image (1210, 355)): a sticker, a light pipe, or a switch access point **(guess: switch/LED access)**.

### 2.3 Upper front face (UF)
- **Shape**: a steep facet spanning the box width (76 mm), from the top's front edge (y +22, z 26) down to the crease CR (y ≈ +34, z ≈ 11), so it slopes at about **52°** and is about **19 mm** long along the slope.
- **Holes HL**: **two round holes**, **Ø 4 mm**, at x ≈ ±19, about mid-height on this face (image (660, 430) and (1355, 430)). Dark inside; the right one (image right, robot left) shows a raised ring (a screw boss or countersink). They are **screw holes / mounting points** for a removable front part, or **access holes** for the wedge's fixing screws **(guess)**.

### 2.4 Lower front wedge (LF) and crease (CR)
- **Shape**: a shallow facet from the crease (z ≈ 11) down to a **sharp leading edge on the floor** at y ≈ +42, sloping at about **25°**. It is the **widest part of the robot**: about **100 mm** at the leading edge, wider than the box (76) so that it covers the **front wheels**; its side ends are cut at about 75° (sloping in toward the top) and come down to the floor at the outer corners (image x 60 and 1960).
- **Crease CR**: the line between UF and LF is a clean horizontal edge across the box width; at the sides it continues into the flared parts.
- **Seam SM**: at the centre of the lower lip, a **vertical raised seam/rib** about **6 mm wide**, running from the crease to the floor (image (1000–1060, 640–760)): an overlap of tape strips, a print seam, or a thin centre rib **(guess: tape overlap / print seam)**.
- **Leading edge**: slightly ragged (chipped corners at the left and right ends), resting on the floor along its full width.
- **Construction (guess)**: the lower lip may be a **separate printed or sheet part** (the faint horizontal line along it suggests a joint), screwed through the two UF holes.

### 2.5 Wheel notches (WN) and wheels (WH)
- **Notches**: rectangular cut-outs at the four corners of the box, each about **20 mm wide (X) × 30 mm long (Y)**, open to the outside and the top, so the wheels sit in the box footprint (spec: "inboard").
- **Wheels**: **black rubber/foam wheels**, about **Ø 26 mm × 18 mm wide**, squat and slightly rounded at the edges (like foam tyres), with a faint blue tint (cut-out halo). They fill their notches; the tyre tops are about **level with the top face** (z ≈ 26), so the robot can drive inverted.
- **Positions**: centres at **x = ±40, y = +16 (front) and y = −30 (rear), z = 13**.
- **Front wheels vs wedge**: the front wheels are **behind** the flared lower lip, which covers their lower front.
- **Drive**: four N20 gearmotors, one per wheel, or two motors with belts **(guess)**.

### 2.6 Internal rib lattice (RB)
- **Shape**: dark lines in the translucent skin forming a **triangular truss**: on the top face, diagonal lines at about ±60° between parallel lines across the width; on the front, a similar zig-zag pattern. These are the **internal walls** of the print (a "triangles" infill pattern at very low density, or deliberately modelled ribs) seen through the 1 mm skin **(medium confidence)**.

## 6. Mechanism

1. **Drive**: four wheels, skid steer (4WD), tops level with the top, so it drives either way up; the wedge works only one way up, but the body shape is a box either way.
2. **Weapon**: none. The robot is a **pure wedge/pusher**: it gets under opponents with the full-width lip and pushes them into walls or out of the arena.

---

## 7. Assembly order (suggested rebuild)

1. Print the box (walls, floor, front facets, wheel notches, internal triangular ribs, motor pockets) in translucent grey.
2. Install four gearmotors with their shafts into the notches; fit the wheels.
3. Install the battery, ESC(s), receiver and switch.
4. Close the top (screwed with four button screws), or print the top as part of the box and use the screws to hold an internal frame.
5. Fit the lower lip if separate (two screws through the UF holes).
6. Wrap everything in clear packing tape.

## 13. Consistency check: conflicts and reconciliation
1. **Spec 62 × 150**: 150 mm exceeds the cube; it is likely a misread of the photo scale. The robot must fit in 101.6 mm: **width 100 (lip), box 76, length 84**.
2. **Front profile**: the low camera angle makes the upper face look tall and the lip short; a 25° lip would need 24 mm of run below an 11 mm crease, making the robot too long. **Reconciled** in §10.1: crease at z 9, upper facet 65°, lip 37°.
3. **Wheels**: inboard notches (spec) and photo agree; diameter matches the 26 mm body height so the robot is invertible.
4. **Rib lattice**: visible lines are consistent with internal ribs, not tape; they continue across tape boundaries.

## 14. Why the designer did it (per part, adversarial first)

- **Full-width flared lip**
  - Fight: the lip is wider than everything else, so whatever the opponent is, the **first contact is the lip's edge on the floor**: it gets under wedges' sides, wheels and flat fronts. Flaring over the front wheels means an opponent attacking at an angle cannot reach the tyres. Against **vertical spinners**, the lip and the steep upper face deflect hits upward; against **horizontal spinners**, the low wide lip lets the spinner's blade ride over.
  - Being got under: a printed lip covered in tape is thicker than steel; a steel-wedged opponent can still get under it. Tape smooths the edge.
- **Two-facet front**
  - Fight: the lower lip gets under; the steep upper facet then acts as a wall to push opponents along rather than letting them ride over the top.
- **Low, flat box**
  - Fight: little to hit; a flat top lets flippers and lifters slide off; the top is level with the wheel tops so it drives inverted (it is a box, it does not care which way up, except that the lip is then upside down).
- **Wheels in corner notches**
  - Fight: wheels inside the footprint are protected from the sides; four-wheel drive gives the most pushing traction for a pusher. Wide foam tyres grip well.
- **Clear tape everywhere**
  - Fight: tape stops spinner teeth from biting into the PLA skin (they skate off the slick tape), holds cracked pieces together after hits, and reduces friction so opponents slide up the wedge.
- **Hollow print with triangular ribs**
  - Construction: a stiff, light shell (triangles are the stiffest truss); the translucent filament shows the structure, a cosmetic touch.
- **Domed screws**
  - Construction: low-profile heads that opponents and spinners slide over; quick access.

## 16. Mass budget (independent check of the size estimates)

A pure wedge has the most weight to spare of any antweight, so the budget tells us how thick the shell can be and whether the reconciled sizes make sense.

| Item | Estimate (g) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 4 × N20 gearmotors | 40 | 10 g each |
| 4 × foam wheels Ø 26 × 18 with printed hubs | 16 | 4 g each |
| Battery 2S 450 mAh | 25 | typical pack |
| ESCs + receiver + switch + wiring | 15 | two dual brushed ESCs, micro RX |
| Screws (4 button M3 + motor/lid screws) | 5 | — |
| Packing tape (about 0.5 m² of 50 µm tape over all faces, several layers) | 8 | 0.05 mm × 1.0 g/cm³ × area |
| Printed shell | 41 | the remainder up to 150 g |
| **Total** | **150** | |

The shell budget of about 41 g of PLA (1.24 g/cm³) is 33 cm³ of plastic. The box's outer surface (top 76 × 84, front facets, sides, rear, floor, lip) is about 230 cm²; at a 1 mm skin that uses 23 cm³, leaving about 10 cm³ for the internal triangular ribs, the motor pockets and the lip's extra thickness. That is consistent with the visible **thin translucent skin over a sparse rib lattice**, and confirms that the robot is about **100 × 84 × 26** rather than the spec's 150 mm dimension (a 150 mm body would need roughly 60 g for the shell alone, too heavy with four motors).

## 17. Surface, wear and damage reading (what the scratches say about the fights)

- **Lower lip**: the densest scratching is on the lip, in **all directions**, with the leading edge **chipped at both outer corners** (image left at (80, 740) and right at (1920, 760)). Multi-directional scratches mean the lip was driven under opponents from many angles and scraped along the floor; the chipped corners are where the lip met walls and opponents' corners. This is the **main contact surface**, as designed.
- **Upper facet**: long **diagonal scratches** run from top-left to bottom-right across the facet (image (420–1100, 360–560)), the kind left by an opponent sliding **up and across** the front after being lifted: evidence that opponents climbed the lip and were deflected by the steeper face.
- **Top face**: lighter, mostly straight scratches front-to-back, typical of opponents or arena hazards passing over the top after the robot was flipped, or of being driven over by a wedge; the domed screws show no damage, so nothing caught on them.
- **Centre seam of the lip**: slightly lifted and creased (image (1000–1060, 640–760)), where the tape overlaps; it is the one place where an opponent's edge could start peeling the tape.
- **Wheels**: the black tyres look intact and clean-edged, so the notches protected them: no side hits reached the tyres.
- **Holes in the upper facet**: the left hole (robot right) is clean, the right hole (robot left) has a raised ring, perhaps a screw head sunk flush or a heat-set insert; both are below the slope surface so an opponent sliding up does not catch.

## 18. Alternative interpretations considered

1. **The front could be a hinged flap** (a "flail wedge" free to float on the floor). Against: the crease between the facets is crisp and continuous across the whole width, and the tape runs smoothly over it; no hinge knuckles or gaps are visible. **Rejected; fixed lip.**
2. **The dark lines could be marker drawings or tape folds** rather than internal ribs. Against: they are straight, regularly spaced, meet at consistent angles, continue under the tape edges and appear on both the top and the front with the same pitch (about 25–30 mm). **Accepted as internal ribs.**
3. **The robot could have only two driven wheels** with two idlers. The four wheels are identical and all sit in matching notches; a pusher benefits from 4WD. **4WD preferred (medium).**
4. **The white square could be a light pipe** (an LED showing through the translucent print would make the whole robot glow). Possible; either reading fits a 4 mm square near the switch area.
